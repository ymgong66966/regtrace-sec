# Adaptive RegTrace Construction Report

This report is generated by the executable RegTrace construction probe. It treats dataset construction as a framework decision rather than as a manual pre-processing step.

## Recursive Construction Loop

1. Inspect available fields and infer review-trace roles.
2. Map local fields into request, response, evidence, label, feedback, and external-corroboration roles.
3. Score whether the corpus has enough observability, evidence, label viability, feedback richness, release viability, and optimization suitability.
4. If the score is weak, reshape the task rather than forcing the SEC schema.
5. Emit a frozen TraceSpec that downstream reviewers and optimizers must obey.

## SEC comment-letter review

- Task: visible-evidence response-resolution review
- Decision: `go-primary-benchmark`
- Instances: 472
- Mean utility: 4.57 / 5

### Role Mapping

- Request: sec_comment
- Response: company_response
- Evidence: retrieved_snippets, amended_evidence_best_snippet
- Label: visible_evidence_resolution_label
- Feedback: scalar_feedback, category_feedback, full_feedback, feedback_sec_request, feedback_company_action, feedback_evidence_summary, feedback_unmet_requirement
- External corroboration: real_followup_text, has_real_followup

### Label Policy

resolved iff the visible amended-filing evidence substantially satisfies the material SEC request; unresolved iff a material requested item, quantification, exhibit, document, legal/accounting analysis, or other obligation is still missing from the visible evidence.

### Feedback Policy

- Scalar: binary correctness against visible_evidence_resolution_label
- Category: issue_category plus coarse gap/category feedback
- Full text: natural-language feedback describing the SEC request, company action, visible evidence coverage, and unmet requirement
- External text: later same-topic SEC follow-up text, used only as noisy corroboration
- Forbidden at test time: feedback_*, full_feedback, amended_evidence_* adjudication fields, real_followup_text

### Distributions

- Labels: `{"unresolved": 319, "resolved": 153}`
- Categories: `{"other": 183, "risk_factor": 91, "spac_disclosure": 78, "revenue_recognition": 75, "crypto": 28, "non_gaap": 16, "cybersecurity": 1}`

### Strengths

- Request, response, amended evidence, label, feedback, and follow-up roles are all observable.
- Natural-language feedback is aligned with the reasoning failures the reviewer must learn.
- 236 examples have later same-topic SEC follow-up for external corroboration.

### Risks

- Labels are visible-evidence judgments and do not replace expert legal/accounting review.
- Retrieval-conditioned labels can be affected by missing amended-filing evidence.

### Next Action

Use as the primary benchmark and report retrieval-conditioned limitations.

## CMS-2567 plan-of-correction review

- Task: deficiency-to-plan adequacy review
- Decision: `go-cross-regulatory-adaptation`
- Instances: 500
- Mean utility: 3.86 / 5

### Role Mapping

- Request: deficiency_text
- Response: plan_of_correction_text
- Evidence: plan_of_correction_text, covered_elements, missing_elements
- Label: cms_poc_binary_label, cms_poc_adequacy_label
- Feedback: cms_poc_optimizer_feedback, cms_poc_missing_elements, cms_poc_covered_elements, cms_poc_reason
- External corroboration: deficiency_corrected, correction_date, days_to_correction

### Label Policy

adequate iff the plan of correction materially covers resident-specific harm, affected population, systemic prevention, monitoring, responsible party, and credible completion; not_adequate iff material remediation elements are missing or only generic.

### Feedback Policy

- Scalar: binary correctness against cms_poc_binary_label
- Category: ftag_group, severity band, and adequacy subtype
- Full text: covered elements, missing elements, and adjudicated adequacy rationale
- External text: CMS correction metadata, treated as weak outcome metadata rather than gold adequacy
- Forbidden at test time: cms_poc_* label/rationale/feedback fields, deficiency_corrected, correction_date, days_to_correction

### Distributions

- Labels: `{"not_adequate": 367, "adequate": 133}`
- Categories: `{"resident_care_safety": 111, "other": 103, "abuse_neglect": 70, "resident_rights": 65, "care_planning_records": 52, "infection_control": 50, "food_safety": 49}`

### Strengths

- Regulator deficiency and provider corrective plan are directly observable.
- The domain forces the framework to adapt the rubric rather than reuse the SEC checklist verbatim.
- Full-feedback optimization already improves over generic and scalar/category CMS controls.

### Risks

- Correction-date metadata is too skewed to serve as the main adequacy label.
- The plan itself is both response and corrective artifact, so evidence is less separable than SEC amended filings.

### Next Action

Use as the main cross-regulatory adaptation study with CMS-specific rubric induction.

## Paper Implication

The strongest claim is not that one SEC prompt transfers everywhere. The stronger claim is that RegTrace supplies a reusable construction interface: for each domain, it decides what counts as request, response, evidence, label, feedback, and external corroboration, then trains or reshapes the reviewer accordingly. SEC is the primary benchmark, CMS is a positive cross-regulatory adaptation, and FDA is a stress test showing that the framework can reshape a corpus when a direct response channel is not publicly observable.
